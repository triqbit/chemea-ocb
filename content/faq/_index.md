## Frequently Asked Questions

### What distinguishes carbon black from charcoal?

Carbon black is an engineered industrial material produced by controlled, incomplete combustion or thermal decomposition of hydrocarbon feedstocks, designed for reinforcement and pigmentation. Charcoal (and activated carbon) is typically produced from biomass or coal and optimized for fuel or adsorption rather than mechanical reinforcement.

### What are the typical production feedstocks and how does this relate to sustainability?

Most furnace and thermal carbon blacks are made from heavy petroleum‑derived feedstocks or natural gas, with specific feedstock choices and efficiency measures determined by each producer. Sustainability performance (for example, energy efficiency, emissions management, or recovered / circular carbon initiatives) is producer‑specific; relevant data can be shared where manufacturers provide it.

### What are the primary applications?

Carbon black is used mainly for:

- Tire treads, sidewalls, and inner components as a reinforcing filler.
- Mechanical rubber goods such as hoses, belts, seals, and vibration parts.
- Plastics and geomembranes for UV protection and colour.
- Industrial compounds, inks, and coatings where pigmentation, conductivity, or durability are needed.

### How should carbon black be stored and what is the shelf life?

ASTM D8043 defines carbon black as having an **indefinite shelf life** when stored to protect it from liquid water and high humidity; key physical properties such as particle size and structure are not expected to degrade with time. Many producers recommend re‑checking parameters such as moisture content (and, if necessary, iodine number) after extended storage, for example after 12 months at the customer site.

Store carbon black in a dry, covered, well‑ventilated area, away from liquid water, high humidity, and incompatible materials to maintain handling and testing consistency.

### What are the key safe‑handling considerations?

Carbon black should be handled as a nuisance dust with attention to:

- Avoiding dust clouds and ensuring adequate local exhaust ventilation.
- Using appropriate PPE such as respiratory protection where exposure limits may be approached, plus eye and skin protection as recommended in the SDS.
- Avoiding dry sweeping or compressed air for cleanup and managing slip hazards if material becomes wet.

Always follow the specific guidance in the producer SDS for the supplied grade.

### Can carbon black be recycled or used in circular applications?

Recycling and circular use depend strongly on application, local infrastructure, and regulations. Options may include rubber reprocessing, tire recycling technologies, or recovered carbon black from pyrolysis, each with its own performance and regulatory considerations. Customers should review local legislation and end‑use standards before treating any material as "recycled" or "recovered" carbon black.

### Is OCB a manufacturer?

No. Chemea™ Oman Carbon Black (OCB) is a **trading organization**, coordinating supply, documentation, and logistics for carbon black grades produced by qualified third‑party manufacturers. All product specifications, SDS, CoA, and regulatory statements originate from those producers.

### What documentation is available?

For supplied grades, OCB can coordinate:

- Safety Data Sheets (SDS) from the relevant producer.
- Batch Certificates of Analysis (CoA) summarizing key test results.
- Producer regulatory / compliance information where available, such as chemical inventory status or regional declarations.

Availability and scope depend on the specific producer and grade.

### What information should be included in an RFQ?

To enable efficient evaluation and quotation, include at minimum:

- Intended application (for example, tire tread, EPDM profile, pressure pipe, geomembrane).
- Target grade or reference standard if known.
- Quantity and expected frequency, packaging preference, and destination.
- Preferred Incoterms and required delivery timeline.
- Documentation and compliance needs (SDS, CoA, regulatory statements, specific standards to reference).
