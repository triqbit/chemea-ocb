## Sustainability in Carbon Black: Building a Circular Economy Strategy

Corporate sustainability commitments increasingly require carbon black procurement teams to evaluate recovered carbon black (rCB) and implement circular economy practices. This article provides a strategic framework for integrating sustainability without compromising performance, cost, or compliance.

### The Sustainability Imperative

**Corporate ESG Targets**:
- Tire manufacturers: 25–30% recovered materials by 2030
- Rubber goods producers: 15–20% circular sourcing by 2028
- Plastics compounds: 10–15% recycled content by 2030

**Regulatory Drivers**:
- **EU Circular Economy Action Plan**: Mandatory recovered content targets
- **Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM)**: Carbon pricing favors low-emission sources
- **Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR)**: Incentives for circular material use
- **Sustainable Finance Taxonomy**: ESG reporting requirements for investors

**Market Reality**: Major OEMs (Michelin, Continental, Goodyear, Bridgestone) have committed to recovered carbon black adoption by 2030.

### Understanding Recovered Carbon Black (rCB)

#### What is Recovered Carbon Black?

Recovered carbon black is carbon black recovered from end-of-life tires and other rubber waste through thermal or chemical processing.

**Production Methods**:
- **Pyrolysis**: Heating waste tires in oxygen-free environment (most common, ~70% of market)
- **Devulcanization**: Chemical breaking of sulfur bonds, recovering active carbon
- **Gasification**: High-temperature conversion of rubber to synthesis gas

**Output Classification** (per ASTM D8474):
- **rCB-P**: From passenger car tires
- **rCB-T**: From truck tires
- **rCB-O**: From other rubber sources
- **Purity grades**: Standard, Enhanced, Premium

#### rCB vs. Virgin Carbon Black: Performance Comparison

| Property | Virgin CB | Recovered CB | Gap | Notes |
|----------|-----------|--------------|-----|-------|
| **Surface Area (m²/g)** | 80–120 | 70–110 | ~10% lower | Modern pyrolysis narrows gap |
| **Iodine Number** | 75–126 | 65–115 | ~8% lower | Application-dependent acceptance |
| **DBP Absorption** | 90–130 | 80–120 | ~7% lower | Structure slightly different |
| **Tensile Strength** | Baseline | 95–105% | 0–5% lower | Often acceptable in testing |
| **Abrasion Resistance** | Baseline | 90–98% | 2–10% lower | Grade/blend dependent |
| **Tear Resistance** | Baseline | 92–99% | 1–8% lower | Usually acceptable |
| **Cost** | Baseline | 70–85% | 15–30% lower | Primary advantage |

**Key Finding**: rCB is 5–10% lower in performance but 15–30% cheaper; blending strategies can offset performance gap.

### Strategic Approaches to rCB Integration

#### Approach 1: Non-Critical Applications (Conservative)

**Suitable For**: Sidewalls, inner liners, non-safety-critical components

**Implementation**:
- Replace 100% of virgin CB with rCB in specific compounds
- Requires ~5–10% formulation adjustment to offset performance gap
- Maintain existing equipment and processes
- Minimal testing complexity

**Blend Target**: 15–25% rCB in overall portfolio

**Performance Risk**: Low  
**Cost Savings**: 8–12%  
**Timeline to Deployment**: 3–6 months  

**Example**: Tire sidewall compound
- Traditional: 100% virgin N-550
- rCB blend: 100% rCB-P (N-550 equivalent from recovered process)
- Result: 5–8% cost reduction, acceptable performance in sidewall (lower stress environment)

---

#### Approach 2: Partial Blending (Moderate)

**Suitable For**: Tread, mid-level performance components

**Implementation**:
- Blend virgin + recovered CB (e.g., 70% virgin / 30% rCB)
- Carefully controlled formulation to maintain performance targets
- Enhanced testing (100+ hours per batch)
- Supplier co-development for consistent rCB quality

**Blend Target**: 25–35% rCB in overall portfolio

**Performance Risk**: Medium (requires validation)  
**Cost Savings**: 5–10%  
**Timeline to Deployment**: 6–12 months  

**Example**: Tire tread formulation
- Traditional: 100% virgin N-330
- rCB blend: 70% virgin N-330 + 30% rCB (sourced from truck tire recycling)
- Result: 6–8% cost reduction, maintained performance in accelerated abrasion testing (ASTM D1630)

**Testing Requirements**:
- ✓ ASTM D412 (tensile strength)
- ✓ ASTM D624 (tear resistance)
- ✓ ASTM D1630 (abrasion resistance)
- ✓ Vehicle-level tire testing (500+ km endurance, wet grip, fuel efficiency)

---

#### Approach 3: Advanced Integration (Aggressive)

**Suitable For**: Specialized applications, innovation-forward organizations

**Implementation**:
- Develop proprietary rCB blends and formulations
- Partnership with rCB producers on process optimization
- Advanced testing and modeling
- Possible use of virgin CB alternatives (e.g., bio-based)

**Blend Target**: 35–50% rCB in overall portfolio

**Performance Risk**: High (requires extensive R&D)  
**Cost Savings**: 10–18%  
**Timeline to Deployment**: 18–36 months  

**Example**: Next-gen sustainable tire
- Proprietary blend: 40% rCB-Enhanced + 50% virgin N-330 + 10% bio-based filler
- Result: 15% cost reduction, 20% lifecycle carbon reduction, market differentiation
- Requires: Vehicle OEM approval, premium market positioning, brand commitment

### Regulatory & Compliance Framework

#### ASTM D8474 Certification

**What it means**: rCB producer has met standardized classification and testing protocols

**Key Requirements**:
- ✓ Process documentation and traceability
- ✓ ASTM test method compliance (D1510, D2414, D6556, etc.)
- ✓ Batch certification and CoA
- ✓ Contaminant screening (heavy metals, sulfur content)

#### EU Circular Economy Requirements

**CBAM (Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism)**:
- Carbon pricing on imports (including carbon black)
- rCB/circular materials receive favorable pricing

**EPR (Extended Producer Responsibility)**:
- Manufacturer responsibility for end-of-life product management
- rCB adoption counts toward EPR targets

**Sustainable Finance Taxonomy**:

| Risk | Mitigation |
|------|-----------|
| **Performance variability** | Strict ASTM D8474 certification, enhanced QC testing, supplier scorecards |
| **Supply inconsistency** | Multi-supplier strategy, long-term contracts, safety stock |
| **Cost premium** | Modern rCB pricing competitive; lock rates early |
| **OEM/regulatory acceptance** | Early engagement with customers, proactive documentation, industry collaboration |
| **Brand risk** | Transparent communication, verified performance data, third-party certifications |
